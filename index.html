Top 5 Contractor Complaints

Common GC Complaints of Subcontractors on Healthcare and Large Commercial Projects.

  1. Safety Violations: Strict safety regulations are in place to protect workers and prevent contamination. Disregarding safety protocols creates hazards and can lead to fines or shutdowns.
  2. Schedule Delays: Strict timelines are critical in healthcare projects. Delays by subcontractors can have a domino effect, impacting the entire project, especially licensing and patient care.
  3. Quality of Workmanship: High quality is essential due to infection control and patient safety. Substandard work can lead to rework, delays, and safety hazards, particularly with specialized systems.
  4. Lack of Communication: Clear communication is key for coordination, and subcontractors who fail to communicate delays, issues, or changes cause disruptions. Proper paperwork and meeting attendance are also crucial.
  5. Failure to Adhere to Contractual Obligations: Healthcare projects have strict contract requirements. Issues with documentation, insurance, change orders, and staffing levels can cause significant problems.

Note: These issues are often interconnected. For example, poor communication can lead to schedule delays and quality problems.

Regularly review and update this Quality Control Plan as needed.

The site management team shall maintain a set of redlines built on the site for review by the project team at any time.  Duct download drawings will be marked up weekly, scanned, and sent back to the Tech Air CAD coordinator for the project for incorporation of any changes into the project model.  Redlines will be provided to Yates as applicable.

Any variation Tech Air seeks will be handled by either the submittal or the RFI process.  No variation shall commence until proper approvals are obtained from all relevant parties.  All documentation of variations will be maintained and available within the Tech Air project management platform.

Submittal Procedure and Submittal Register

Tech Air shall develop and complete the submittal register listing all required submittals per the contract documents (Specifications and Drawings). The submittal register shall include the item description, the transmittal date, the return date, and the appropriate action classification (approved, approved as noted, etc.). Operations and Maintenance Manuals (O & M)

Operation and Maintenance Manuals should be received before the equipment arrives on site and will be submitted and approved before any system or equipment tests are performed. Equipment O&M Manuals provide for but are not necessarily limited to the step-by-step start-up and operation, operating instructions, detailed drawings, emergency procedures, warranties, maintenance procedures, information for the data package specified, and other turnover documentation required by the specifications. Equipment O & M Manuals shall be obtained, submitted, approved, and utilized for installation, including rigging, handling & storage, training, and commissioning of life safety dampers and access doors. NETA is not responsible for any additional equipment on this project. Therefore, all O&M manuals associated with any extra equipment are to be obtained and distributed by SPC.

Final copies will be provided before substantial completion, beneficial occupancy, or any specified instruction period. No Operator Training will be performed until O & M Manuals have been approved.

All employees shall be trained in the installation instructions for all equipment before the work commences.  Any questions or issues regarding the proper installation of equipment will be directed to the equipment manufacturer and documented via the project documentation procedures described above.

Performance and Inspection

The Quality Control Plan's performance and inspection section outlines the work's expectations in progress and defines the in-process inspection. The work in progress is expected to perform each task properly, with the best workmanship by the design documents. To achieve this expectation, the supervisor and project staff shall initiate a work plan for each definable feature of work, including but not limited to:

  • The drawings, plans, and specs made available
  • Sequence of work reviewed
  • Coordination Drawings available
  • Work method and schedule reviewed
  • The duct Leak Testing Plan has been reviewed, and the testing sequence has been established.
  • The submittals have been approved
  • Material/Equipment scheduled, released and delivered
  • Material/Equipment receipt inspections are complete
  • Safety – job hazard analysis reviewed with installers

During construction, the quality team, led by the site Quality Control Supervisor, will perform in-process inspections to verify compliance with the design documents, codes, and acceptable industry practices. As material arrives at the site or the warehouse from either the NETA shop or 3rd party manufacturers, it shall be inventoried for compliance with the approved submittals and project documents.  Digital cameras and iPads will be used to document results.

  • The craftsmen perform the first run studies to ensure accuracy and completeness (i.e., verifying layouts, sizes, and quantities installed).
  • The Foreman’s responsibility is to reinforce the established quality requirements, provide training for specific tasks, and ensure each craftsman is qualified to perform the work and meet the expectations.  The Foreman shall inspect the job daily, guaranteeing the workmanship quality meets expectations and is ready for examination and/or testing. The Foreman documents the results of his inspections on the forms provided, noting any deficiencies.
  • The superintendent is responsible for communicating with the foreman about the work plan and inspection criteria, performing the necessary work reviews, and performing the various daily inspections. The Superintendent is to verify all completed work is ready for examination and/or testing.  The Superintendent shall be responsible for releasing any areas for further construction and compliance with coordination drawings/design documents. The Superintendent shall record all deficiencies in the Deficiency Log (see Appendix) to track necessary corrective action.
  • The Project Manager is responsible for the overall project work plan and delegates the responsibilities to his Superintendents and/or Foremen team.  
  • The Site Quality Control Supervisor shall act as the overall quality assurance for the various work features, which includes the organization of work plans and compliance with the various in-process inspections, as well as documenting results in the Tech Air internal project management platform.

Quality will be discussed during this meeting, and results will be documented on the Weekly Quality Control Report (see Appendix); weekly staff meeting minutes are to be distributed to the Executive Assistant.

  • Record number of tests performed and the Accept/Reject rate
  • Deficiencies Identified during the week, a deficiency fixed the same day discovered does not mean it is not relevant; review added deficiencies to Yates Punch List Tracking System

Final Acceptance Inspection

The Project Manager is responsible for scheduling the Final Acceptance Inspection after all items identified in the Pre-Final Inspection are complete and accepted. The Site Quality Control Supervisor or Superintendent will conduct their Final Inspection and input defective items in the “Punch List.” Punch List items are required to be completed in writing. The process will be repeated until Final Acceptance is achieved.  NETA will participate in the Cleveland County project turnover procedures and will complete all punch list items in EIDA per the project requirements.

Any items found to be non-conforming to approved submittals, RFI, or other contract documents will be reported immediately to the NETA management along with SPC, and Yates.  NETA will remedy all issues that fall within the scope of their contract.

Pre-Construction Quality Activities      

Pre-construction activities occur before commencing physical construction at the job site. These pre-construction activities frequently revolve around project budgets, and decisions made at this stage can significantly impact constructability and quality performance. Tech Air will coordinate its pre-construction services with Owners, Architects, Engineers, JE Dunn, SPC, and other consultants.

The following paragraphs describe the pre-construction activities as they relate to Quality.

Design Peer Reviews

CM will collaborate with Owners to determine when peer reviews may improve the process. The purpose is to assess the design documents' adequacy, integration, completeness, code compliance, and conformance with manufacturer, supplier, and vendor requirements, including warranties. Possible peer review consultants may include but are not limited to structural, MEP, acoustic, vibration, waterproofing/roofing, lighting, ADA, or code compliance.

Constructability Reviews

The CM and trade partners will provide constructability reviews to be performed by Superintendents, PMs, and Estimators collaboratively with Owners and project consultants. Early involvement by the project team during these reviews will reduce the likelihood of incorporating products or designs prone to installation or functional difficulties. Extra emphasis should be placed on reviews of high-risk, one-of-a-kind, or unusual design elements at both the DD and CD design stages.

Review of Plans and Specifications

Before beginning construction, the CM superintendents and PMs will review the contract documents in depth. This review will identify conflicts, unclear or incomplete items, etc. A detailed list of comments and clarifications will be developed to openly communicate our understanding and interpretations of the design.

Documenting Existing Conditions (if applicable)

The CM shall inspect and create a photographic and/or video record that documents existing conditions, paying particular attention to preexisting physical defects. Items to be photographed include

  • Existing structures within or directly adjacent to the construction limits are to remain.
  • Adjacent buildings and structures outside the construction limits which may be affected by construction activities
  • Adjacent roads, sidewalks, curbs, paving, and drainage structures
  • Vegetation
  • Above-ground utilities

Samples, Submittals, and Procurement

The CM will utilize a comprehensive submittal log to provide and approve all submittals and samples.  The submittals will be reviewed for compliance, and notes for QA will be added to the QA Checklists in each trade contractor’s QMP.

Submittal samples shall demonstrate the quality level acceptable to the design team. When specified, this may be a mock-up outside of the actual area of construction, or in other cases, it may be the first acceptable unit of production. These sample mock-ups will constitute an acceptable level of workmanship.  NETA procurement of all materials will be based on the approved submittals for the project.

Factory/Shop Inspections

As part of the pre-construction planning process, the Project Team may elect to tour manufacturing assemblies or other projects to confirm the quality of a product and/or installation.  This will be documented and tracked on the OPS.

Material Receiving at the Jobsite

Trade partners' SQCS or designee will verify that 100% of the items delivered to the job site conform with approved submittals and specifications upon receipt. NETA will utilize forms provided by JE Dunn when receiving materials at the job site.  All materials will be inspected for damage and conform to approved submittals.  Any materials found to be deficient will be removed or replaced. SPC provides all equipment; therefore, SPC will be responsible for any equipment found to be deficient.

Testing Specification

General

  1. All ductwork that is required to be tested shall be tested on regular intervals as the job proceeds and shall be completed prior to enclosure in shafts, above ceilings or behind walls.
  2. The Sheet Metal Contractor shall keep an up-to-date log of the ductwork tested for review by the Architect. The test records shall include marked drawings showing the system/area tested. The testing system/area shall be designated with unique naming. Testing documentation shall include pressure class, system, dates, calculations, allowable leakage, tested leakage, and signatures. Testing results shall be Pass or Fail.
  3. The Sheet Metal Contractor shall notify all other Contractors when the testing is completed and accepted to permit the enclosure of ducts. All test documentation shall be submitted as part of the project close-out documentation package.
  4. The Sheet Metal Contractor shall furnish and install all blank off plates, blind flanges, saving, etc., necessary to isolate each section of duct being tested for leakage.
  5. The Sheet Metal Contractor shall submit for review all proposed testing procedures.
  6. All test equipment shall be calibrated per ANSI Standards prior to testing.  Copies of certifications will be provided to JE Dunn as applicable.

  9. The test pressure shall be the specified construction pressure of the duct system.
